Wǎnxī (婉兮) — Grace itself, from the oldest Chinese poetry

Wǎnxī · 婉兮（婉兮）

Soft, graceful, and lovely. Wǎnxī is the very word a traveler in the Book of Songs uses when he falls in love at first sight on a dewy morning.

野有蔓草，零露漙兮。有美一人，清扬婉兮。
“Wild grasses trail in the field, dew lies thick. There is a lovely one — bright-eyed and softly graceful.”
— 《诗经·郑风·野有蔓草》 (Book of Songs), c. 600 BCE

What Wǎnxī means

Soft, graceful, and lovely. Wǎnxī is the very word a traveler in the Book of Songs uses when he falls in love at first sight on a dewy morning.

The poem behind the name

The line comes from 《诗经·郑风·野有蔓草》 (Book of Songs), c. 600 BCE. In the original: “野有蔓草，零露漙兮。有美一人，清扬婉兮。”. Wild grasses trail in the field, dew lies thick. There is a lovely one — bright-eyed and softly graceful.

The characters, idea by idea

婉 is built from the woman radical (女) and 宛 'as if, gently curved' — grace that bends like a willow without breaking. 兮 is an ancient exclamation particle, pure music in a character.

Who this name suits

Wǎnxī suits someone gentle with an unmistakable inner strength. The name reads as poetry itself — ideal for a girl, and beloved by writers and artists for its sound.

Common Questions

Is Wǎnxī a common Chinese name?

Almost never seen as a modern legal name; it lives in poetry, usernames, and art. That rarity makes it feel discovered rather than chosen.

How do you pronounce 婉兮?

It is pronounced “wahn-SHEE” in Mandarin (Wǎnxī with tone marks). Non-speakers can say it as written and Chinese friends will understand and smile.

Can a foreigner use 婉兮 as a Chinese name?

Yes — and it is a beautiful choice. Unlike translated names, 婉兮 is a real poetic name with a story you can tell in one sentence. 婉兮 is written identically in simplified and traditional Chinese. The particle 兮 has been untranslated for 2,500 years — it is simply the sound of the old songs.
