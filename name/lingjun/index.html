Língjūn (灵均) — The courtesy name of China's first great poet

Língjūn · 灵均（靈均）

Spirit balanced, grace held steady. Língjūn is the courtesy name Qu Yuan chose to carry through his life's work — elegance with equilibrium.

名余曰正则兮，字余曰灵均。
“My father named me Zhèngzé, and gave me the courtesy name Língjūn.”
— 《楚辞·离骚》 (Li Sao), c. 300 BCE

What Língjūn means

Spirit balanced, grace held steady. Língjūn is the courtesy name Qu Yuan chose to carry through his life's work — elegance with equilibrium.

The poem behind the name

The line comes from 《楚辞·离骚》 (Li Sao), c. 300 BCE. In the original: “名余曰正则兮，字余曰灵均。”. My father named me Zhèngzé, and gave me the courtesy name Língjūn.

The characters, idea by idea

灵 (靈) holds rain (雨), three mouths (口), and a shaman (巫) — the character for spirit, numen, and brilliant wit. 均 levels a field with its earth radical (土): equal, balanced, just.

Who this name suits

Língjūn suits someone brilliant yet grounded — the artist with discipline, the scholar with charm. Refined and unisex, with a poet's aura.

Common Questions

Is Língjūn a common Chinese name?

灵 is a popular modern name element; 灵均 as a full name is rare outside literary circles, which keeps it exclusive.

How do you pronounce 灵均?

It is pronounced “ling-JOON” in Mandarin (Língjūn with tone marks). Non-speakers can say it as written and Chinese friends will understand and smile.

Can a foreigner use 灵均 as a Chinese name?

Yes — and it is a beautiful choice. Unlike translated names, 灵均 is a real poetic name with a story you can tell in one sentence. Simplified 灵均 / traditional 靈均. 靈 is one of the most visually rich characters in Chinese — rain, mouths, and a shaman calling the spirits.
