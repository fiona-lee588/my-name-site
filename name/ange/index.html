Āngē (安歌) — To sing, unhurried, at your own pace

Āngē · 安歌（安歌）

To sing at ease. In the Chu festival of the sun, the goddess sets a slow rhythm and sings without hurry — the ancient Chinese image of a life at its own pace.

What Āngē means

To sing at ease. In the Chu festival of the sun, the goddess sets a slow rhythm and sings without hurry — the ancient Chinese image of a life at its own pace.

The poem behind the name

The line comes from 《楚辞·九歌·东君》 (Songs of Chu, 'The Lord of the East'), c. 300 BCE. In the original: “疏缓节兮安歌，陈竽瑟兮浩倡。”. Beating a slow, unhurried rhythm, she sings at ease; yu pipes and se zithers rise in great chorus.

The characters, idea by idea

安 shows a woman (女) under a roof (宀) — the oldest Chinese picture of safety and contentment. 歌 joins 哥 'older brother/song' with 欠 'open mouth' — singing itself.

Who this name suits

Āngē suits someone calm and confident, who moves to their own rhythm and never rushes the people they love. Gentle for a girl, poised for a boy, soothing for anyone.

Common Questions

Is Āngē a common Chinese name?

安 is one of the most beloved name characters in modern China; 安歌 as a combination is rare, keeping the familiar comfort of 安 with a poetic twist.

How do you pronounce 安歌?

It is pronounced “ahn-GUH” in Mandarin (Āngē with tone marks). Non-speakers can say it as written and Chinese friends will understand and smile.

Can a foreigner use 安歌 as a Chinese name?

Yes — and it is a beautiful choice. Unlike translated names, 安歌 is a real poetic name with a story you can tell in one sentence. 安歌 is the same in both scripts. It is one of the few names that translates perfectly into a life philosophy: 'sing at ease.'
