Yúnqí (云旗) — Banners of cloud on the sun goddess's chariot

Yúnqí · 云旗（雲旗）

Banners of flowing cloud. The Songs of Chu dress the sun goddess's chariot in cloud-standards — regal, sky-born, impossible to ignore.

载云旗之委蛇，鸣玉鸾之啾啾。
“Cloud banners streaming and coiling, jade bells ringing all along the way.”
— 《楚辞·九歌·东君》 (Songs of Chu, 'The Lord of the East'), c. 300 BCE

What Yúnqí means

Banners of flowing cloud. The Songs of Chu dress the sun goddess's chariot in cloud-standards — regal, sky-born, impossible to ignore.

The poem behind the name

The line comes from 《楚辞·九歌·东君》 (Songs of Chu, 'The Lord of the East'), c. 300 BCE. In the original: “载云旗之委蛇，鸣玉鸾之啾啾。”. Cloud banners streaming and coiling, jade bells ringing all along the way.

The characters, idea by idea

云 (雲) pictures drifting clouds over a source of breath and vapor. 旗 is the banner itself, with 方 'square standard' and the streaming fringe — a flag that moves.

Who this name suits

Yúnqí suits someone born to stand out — ambitious, bright, and generous with their light. A powerful unisex name, and a natural fit for creative brands.

Common Questions

Is Yúnqí a common Chinese name?

云旗 is occasionally chosen for its grandeur but remains uncommon; it carries the full theatrical splendor of Chu poetry.

How do you pronounce 云旗?

It is pronounced “yoon-CHEE” in Mandarin (Yúnqí with tone marks). Non-speakers can say it as written and Chinese friends will understand and smile.

Can a foreigner use 云旗 as a Chinese name?

Yes — and it is a beautiful choice. Unlike translated names, 云旗 is a real poetic name with a story you can tell in one sentence. Simplified 云旗 / traditional 雲旗. Notice how 雲 keeps the rain radical (雨) on top — the cloud that carries water across the sky.
