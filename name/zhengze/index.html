Zhèngzé (正则) — The name Qu Yuan's father gave him

Zhèngzé · 正则（正則）

Fair and upright, keeping true measure in all things. This is the actual given name Qu Yuan records for himself in China's first great long poem.

名余曰正则兮，字余曰灵均。
“My father named me Zhèngzé, and gave me the courtesy name Língjūn.”
— 《楚辞·离骚》 (Li Sao, 'On Encountering Sorrow'), c. 300 BCE

What Zhèngzé means

Fair and upright, keeping true measure in all things. This is the actual given name Qu Yuan records for himself in China's first great long poem.

The poem behind the name

The line comes from 《楚辞·离骚》 (Li Sao, 'On Encountering Sorrow'), c. 300 BCE. In the original: “名余曰正则兮，字余曰灵均。”. My father named me Zhèngzé, and gave me the courtesy name Língjūn.

The characters, idea by idea

正 is a footprint (止) aiming at a target line (一) — going straight at the mark. 则 (則) joins a ritual vessel (鼎) with a knife: the rule you measure and cut by.

Who this name suits

Zhèngzé suits someone with a strong sense of fairness and precision — an engineer, a judge, a founder who keeps their word. A serious, respected name for a boy.

Common Questions

Is Zhèngzé a common Chinese name?

正则 is rare as a legal name but deeply respected; readers of the Li Sao recognize it immediately as a name with 2,300 years of integrity behind it.

How do you pronounce 正则?

It is pronounced “jeng-DZUH” in Mandarin (Zhèngzé with tone marks). Non-speakers can say it as written and Chinese friends will understand and smile.

Can a foreigner use 正则 as a Chinese name?

Yes — and it is a beautiful choice. Unlike translated names, 正则 is a real poetic name with a story you can tell in one sentence. Simplified 正则 / traditional 正則. 則 is also the character used for 'rule, law, principle' — the name literally means 'the upright standard.'
