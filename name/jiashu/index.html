Jiāshù (嘉树) — The noble tree Qu Yuan praised

Jiāshù · 嘉树（嘉樹）

A noble, blessed tree. Qu Yuan, China's first great poet, praised the orange tree that keeps its roots and stays true through every winter.

后皇嘉树，橘徕服兮。受命不迁，生南国兮。
“O noble tree, the orange, born to the southern land. You received heaven's charge and will not be moved.”
— 《楚辞·九章·橘颂》 (Songs of Chu, 'Hymn to the Orange Tree'), c. 300 BCE

What Jiāshù means

A noble, blessed tree. Qu Yuan, China's first great poet, praised the orange tree that keeps its roots and stays true through every winter.

The poem behind the name

The line comes from 《楚辞·九章·橘颂》 (Songs of Chu, 'Hymn to the Orange Tree'), c. 300 BCE. In the original: “后皇嘉树，橘徕服兮。受命不迁，生南国兮。”. O noble tree, the orange, born to the southern land. You received heaven's charge and will not be moved.

The characters, idea by idea

嘉 means excellent and praiseworthy — it appears in bronze inscriptions over 3,000 years old. 树 (樹) is the tree standing firm; its traditional form holds 木 'wood', 又 'hand', and 寸 'measure' — a tree tended with care.

Who this name suits

Jiāshù suits someone steadfast and quietly excellent — the friend who never moves when the weather turns. A strong, warm name for a boy, and a respected choice for brands and studios.

Common Questions

Is Jiāshù a common Chinese name?

嘉 (jiā) is a classic, still-popular name element; 嘉树 as a full name is uncommon enough to stand out while every Chinese reader finds it instantly dignified.

How do you pronounce 嘉树?

It is pronounced “jah-SHOO” in Mandarin (Jiāshù with tone marks). Non-speakers can say it as written and Chinese friends will understand and smile.

Can a foreigner use 嘉树 as a Chinese name?

Yes — and it is a beautiful choice. Unlike translated names, 嘉树 is a real poetic name with a story you can tell in one sentence. Simplified 嘉树 / traditional 嘉樹. The traditional form shows the full 'wood' family of characters — 樹 is a tree you can almost see being planted.
