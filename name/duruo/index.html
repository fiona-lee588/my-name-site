Dùruò (杜若) — The fragrant herb of river goddesses

Dùruò · 杜若（杜若）

An aromatic herb gathered as an offering to river goddesses — a name for quiet virtue that lingers, like scent on water.

采芳洲兮杜若，将以遗兮下女。
“On the fragrant isle I gather dùruò, to leave for the maiden below.”
— 《楚辞·九歌·湘君》 (Songs of Chu, 'The Goddess of the Xiang'), c. 300 BCE

What Dùruò means

An aromatic herb gathered as an offering to river goddesses — a name for quiet virtue that lingers, like scent on water.

The poem behind the name

The line comes from 《楚辞·九歌·湘君》 (Songs of Chu, 'The Goddess of the Xiang'), c. 300 BCE. In the original: “采芳洲兮杜若，将以遗兮下女。”. On the fragrant isle I gather dùruò, to leave for the maiden below.

The characters, idea by idea

杜 is a wild pear tree, hardy and self-sown. 若 suggests both 'like' and 'to conform' — and in old texts it names sweet-smelling herbs. Together they evoke an unforced, natural grace.

Who this name suits

Dùruò suits someone whose goodness is understated and lasting — the person you appreciate more with every meeting. Gender-neutral, elegant, with a cool botanical freshness.

Common Questions

Is Dùruò a common Chinese name?

Rarely a legal given name today, but well known from poetry and from classic novels — Chinese readers receive it as cultured and refined.

How do you pronounce 杜若?

It is pronounced “doo-ROH” in Mandarin (Dùruò with tone marks). Non-speakers can say it as written and Chinese friends will understand and smile.

Can a foreigner use 杜若 as a Chinese name?

Yes — and it is a beautiful choice. Unlike translated names, 杜若 is a real poetic name with a story you can tell in one sentence. 杜若 is identical in simplified and traditional writing. The herb itself is usually identified with Pollia japonica, still grown in southern Chinese gardens.
