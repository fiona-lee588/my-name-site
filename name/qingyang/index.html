Qīngyáng (清扬) — The oldest compliment to a beautiful person

Qīngyáng · 清扬（清揚）

Bright eyes and a clear brow. In the oldest Chinese poetry, qīngyáng was the highest compliment for a person's face: clear, radiant, impossible to forget.

有美一人，清扬婉兮
“There is a lovely one — bright eyes and a clear, lifted brow.”
— 《诗经·郑风·野有蔓草》 (Book of Songs, 'Wild Grasses'), c. 600 BCE

What Qīngyáng means

Bright eyes and a clear brow. In the oldest Chinese poetry, qīngyáng was the highest compliment for a person's face: clear, radiant, impossible to forget. This is the name behind the poem: “有美一人，清扬婉兮” — “There is a lovely one — bright eyes and a clear, lifted brow.”

The poem behind the name

The line comes from 《诗经·郑风·野有蔓草》 (Book of Songs, 'Wild Grasses'), c. 600 BCE. In the original: “有美一人，清扬婉兮”. There is a lovely one — bright eyes and a clear, lifted brow.

The characters, idea by idea

清 combines the water radical (氵) with 青 'green/blue' — the clarity of pure water. 扬 means to lift and to spread, as wind lifts a banner.

Who this name suits

Qīngyáng suits someone whose presence is quiet but luminous — the friend everyone remembers for their clear gaze. It suits a girl or a woman, and works beautifully as a literary name, a brand, or a baby's name.

Common Questions

Is Qīngyáng a common Chinese name?

It is rare as a modern given name, which is exactly its charm — instantly classical, never ordinary. Chinese readers will recognize the poem within a single glance.

How do you pronounce 清扬?

It is pronounced “cheeng-YAHNG” in Mandarin (Qīngyáng with tone marks). Non-speakers can say it as written and Chinese friends will understand and smile.

Can a foreigner use 清扬 as a Chinese name?

Yes — and it is a beautiful choice. Unlike translated names, 清扬 is a real poetic name with a story you can tell in one sentence. Traditional form 清揚 is used in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and older texts; simplified 清扬 is standard in mainland China. Both are written the same way in pinyin: Qīngyáng.
